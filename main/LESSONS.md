# Lessons from Talha's feedback
What Talha has corrected so far, written as craft that holds across clips. Read this before any clipping or editing, together with CLAUDE.md and the client's rule files. Only Talha adds to this file. A pattern from one video is never a rule.

## Picking clips
- Score every self-contained moment on the four questions, whatever the topic: funny, sad, faith, money, conspiracy, motivation. Filtering by topic is how we missed 8 viral clips once.
- Look for host Q&A, not only monologues. The host's short question is often the hook, and the guest's crisp answer is the payoff. Wide two-shots are fine.
- Read well past the first punchline. End on the line that lands, then let the last word breathe (about 0.3 s of quiet).
- Clip long and let the edit trim. Over 50 s is fine, and raw clips can go over 2 minutes.
- Cut rambles and tangents of any length inside a clip.
- One moment with two payoffs becomes two clips. Two neighbouring clips about one idea can merge.

## Cutting
- Edit word to word, with no dead air. Remove fillers, stutters, false starts and accidental repeats.
- Keep repeats the speaker does on purpose for effect ("$2 trillion… $2 trillion").
- The transcript is not the truth. It invents repeats and misses real ones. Check every drop on the audio before cutting.
- A filler that runs straight into the next word stays; cutting it chops speech.
- After rendering, transcribe the finished clip and compare it with the captions, to catch chopped words.
- Emotional moments keep their pauses. A host-and-answer clip can keep the quiet after the answer.
- The voice stays as recorded. If it peaks too high, turn the whole clip down evenly; never compress.

## B-roll
- Show what he means, not the literal words. "Detonates your home like a grenade" means a family fight, not an explosion. Write the meaning as a real-life situation first, then search for that.
- A literal shot is fine only when the literal image already carries the meaning (a man alone at his window for "no one's waiting at the door").
- Leave a punchline or emotional peak on the speaker when no B-roll would add to it. A B-roll that makes a line look silly is worse than none.
- Lists get one shot per item.
- Use iconic, recognisable characters (Bruce Wayne, Thomas Shelby, Jordan Belfort, Walter White). Search for "<character> 4K scene pack", "4K twixtor" or "4K cinematic".
- 4K sources only: a vertical crop of 1080p looks soft. Twixtor packs are slow motion, so play them at about 2x.
- The character is whole and in the centre of the vertical frame: medium shot or wider, the whole head inside. Use full height and follow them if they move.
- No extreme close-ups, no hands, feet or objects on their own. A wide shot without people is fine when it carries the meaning.
- Real footage, movie scenes or animations. No AI renders, no watermarks, no burned-in captions.
- Each shot sits on its exact phrase and flows; never a 1-second flash between talking-head shots.

## Hooks and copy
- Simple and human, the way a person would say it. Name the speaker only when the hook needs it. Emojis only when they add something.
- Never promise what the clip doesn't pay off.
